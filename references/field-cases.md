# Anonymized Field Cases

These cases encode patterns, not allegations about named people or companies. Do not present them as verified public events.

## Case 1: Subway Screening Fee And Newly Disclosed Certificates

### User-Reported Sequence

1. A student interviewed for a Beijing subway-screening role.
2. The recruiter requested more than CNY 300 for a medical examination.
3. The recruiter said work could be arranged the next day.
4. On arrival, the student was told the role was full and was directed to wait elsewhere.
5. Security-screening and security-guard certificates were disclosed only later.
6. Obtaining them was said to require about half a month without wages.
7. An independent inquiry at a subway station suggested that summer workers were not currently being recruited.

### Patterns

- current vacancy was not verified with the actual work user;
- payment came before role, start date, and prerequisites were confirmed;
- certificates and unpaid waiting were material late disclosures;
- sunk cost and travel made the student more vulnerable to substitute roles;
- the recruiter's claim and independently obtained information conflicted.

### Expected Skill Response

Use `pause and verify` before payment; after the vacancy conflict and material changes, use `stop now` for further payment or travel. Verify the actual employer, role authorization, certificate requirements, pay during training, fee payee, receipt, and refund terms. Preserve the original promise, payment, changed instructions, and independent inquiry.

## Case 2: Security-Company Office With Concealed Identity

### User-Reported Sequence

1. A student arrived at an office associated with a security job.
2. The student could not find a displayed business identity or clear license information.
3. Company names on wall rules appeared crossed out or intentionally obscured.
4. Many student applicants were present.
5. Some people at the office smoked and had tattoos.

### Patterns

Use only items 2-4 as operational observations. Item 5 is not evidence of reliability and must be excluded from the decision.

The decision-relevant issues are concealed or inconsistent company identity, inability to tie rules and documents to a legal entity, unclear role allocation, and mass waiting without a specific confirmed placement.

### Expected Skill Response

Move the student to `pause and verify` or `stop now` if pressure, payment, document retention, unexplained transport, or blocked exit exists. Record the address and exact visible names, leave safely, verify the legal entity and applicable authorization independently, and confirm the specific job with the actual work user before returning.

## Case 3: Referral Reward For Classmates

### User-Reported Pattern

A student who plans to join with classmates may tell the employer they can introduce more students and receive an additional reward.

### Risks

- the student recruits others before personally verifying actual conditions;
- commission comes from recruits, wage deductions, fees, or an endless referral chain;
- the student collects documents or money and becomes part of the recruitment risk;
- classmates reasonably believe the student guaranteed the role;
- a role change, wage dispute, or injury damages both students and relationships.

### Expected Skill Response

Allow discussion only after normal verified entry and written confirmation of the employer-funded referral policy. Require disclosure to classmates, independent review of terms, no guarantees, and no collection of money or identity documents. Reject recruitment as a condition for refund, placement, or wages.

## Case 4: On-Site Bait-And-Switch With Sunk-Cost Trapping

### User-Reported Sequence

1. A student was recruited for a security guard role with a promised wage of CNY 5,500 for 45 days of work.
2. The recruiter collected CNY 700 for insurance, stating it would be refunded if the student completed the 45 days.
3. The student traveled to the site. Upon arrival, the role was described as day-rate only, with work assigned irregularly.
4. Over 13 days at the site, the student worked only 3 days and earned approximately CNY 300.
5. The employer implied that leaving early would forfeit the CNY 700 insurance payment.
6. The student felt trapped: staying produced near-zero income and ongoing living costs; leaving meant losing the CNY 700 and earning almost nothing for the time spent.

### Patterns

- promised fixed-term monthly wage replaced with irregular day-rate with no guaranteed minimum;
- fee originally labeled as insurance relabeled or implied as a penalty for early departure;
- the student's sunk cost (travel and fee) is used as psychological pressure to continue;
- the actual work user and wage payer were not independently confirmed before travel;
- housing or site control creates additional exit barriers.

### Expected Skill Response

Apply the on-site emergency workflow: separate safety check, bait-and-switch detection checklist, and sunk-cost decision framework. Calculate the expected net outcome of staying versus leaving, including continuing living costs and opportunity cost. Advise the student that a near-zero or negative expected net outcome in an uncontrolled environment justifies leaving, even if it means writing off the sunk fee. Preserve evidence of the original promise, the fee payment, the changed work terms, and the actual days worked. Send written clarification and refund requests before departure. Do not recruit classmates or pay additional money to recover the earlier fee. File complaints through the recruiting platform, labor inspection, and legal aid after returning to a safe location.
